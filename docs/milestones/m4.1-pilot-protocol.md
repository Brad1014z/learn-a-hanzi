# M4.1 pilot protocol — First Ten Minutes

**Status: draft for review. Do not enroll a participant until this protocol, the guardian
consent, and the teen assent have been reviewed by the organization running the pilot.**

This protocol turns the external gates in
[`m4.1-release-gates.md`](m4.1-release-gates.md) into a repeatable session. It is written
for a small, facilitated pilot (5–8 beginners ages 12–16), not for a public release.

## 1. The questions the pilot answers

1. Can a complete beginner finish the first core quest **unaided**, in **ten minutes or
   less**?
2. Does the feedback feel **fair** (≥4/5) to the learner?
3. Can the learner **write two of 人/大/天 from memory the next day**?
4. Does the learner **return for a third session within four days**?
5. Is the journey **stable** — no crash, lost progress, duplicate reward, or blocked core
   path?
6. Separately, does the grader meet the **corpus** fairness thresholds (honest false
   rejection <5%, deliberate acceptance <1%)? The corpus is recorded in its own phase; see
   [`data/corpus/README.md`](../../data/corpus/README.md).

## 2. What ships, and what does not

- The pilot artifact is a **signed release APK** built from a `v*` tag, with
  `SOCIAL_ENABLED=false`. There is no account, no sync, no family board, and no network
  dependency for learning.
- **The pilot ships no telemetry.** The release `PilotStrokeExporter` remains a no-op. The
  debug-only stroke exporter is used for the separate corpus phase, not for learner
  measurement.
- Measurement is the **facilitator worksheet** in [`data/pilot/`](../../data/pilot/README.md).
  It contains pseudonymous IDs and only the facts the gate needs.

**Why no telemetry:** the participants are minors; the app is offline-first by
constitution; and a five-to-eight-person pilot does not need a data pipeline. A worksheet
is easier to audit, easier to delete, and cannot leak by accident. If a later pilot needs
telemetry, it needs a new privacy decision and a new protocol — not a quiet build flag.

## 3. Participants

- **5–8 complete beginners**, ages 12–16, comfortable with an Android phone.
- “Complete beginner” means no prior formal Chinese reading instruction. Before
  enrollment, show 人/大/天 on paper and ask whether the learner can read any of them. A
  “yes” means this is not the right pilot; thank them and do not enroll.
- **Guardian consent is required** before any session. **Teen assent is required** in
  addition to guardian consent; a guardian cannot consent for a teen who declines.
- Participation is voluntary. The learner or guardian may stop at any time, for any
  reason, with no penalty and no need to explain.
- Use pseudonymous IDs (`P1`…`P8`). Names, phone numbers, and signed forms never appear in
  the worksheet, the report, or the repository.

## 4. Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Facilitator | Consent/assent, install, session script, observation, worksheet |
| Second adult | Safeguarding presence; can stop a session; does not coach |
| Guardian | Available during the session; may observe or stop |
| Maintainer | Artifact, analysis, gate report; does not coach participants |

A session should not run with one adult alone with a minor. Follow the safeguarding policy
of the school, club, or organization hosting the pilot.

## 5. Artifact and setup

1. Install the signed APK from the release channel. Record the version name/code on the
   worksheet header (or in the facilitator notes).
2. Put the phone in airplane mode / Wi-Fi off for the session. The app must work; if it
   does not, that is a finding, not a reason to enable the network.
3. Reset progress before the first session so the learner starts from a clean state.
   Never reset between sessions; the next-day and return measurements depend on it.
4. Set a comfortable volume and brightness. Confirm the audio plays.
5. One phone per participant. Do not share a phone between participants; progress would
   mix and the measurements would be invalid.

## 6. Session schedule

| Session | When | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| First | Day 0 | Consent/assent, eligibility check (人/大/天), **first core quest**, fairness question, observer notes |
| Second | Day 1 | **Next-day recall** on paper, then the day’s quest (due reviews + up to three new) |
| Third | Days 2–4 | The third session; record the date |

- The third session must be **1–4 days after the first quest**. A same-day “third
  session” is not a return and does not count.
- The next-day recall must be on the **calendar day after** the first quest. If the
  family cannot attend that day, record the actual date; do not coach the learner to make
  the gate.
- Do not add sessions to hit a number. The point is the natural first-ten-minutes
  experience.

## 7. Metric definitions

| Metric | Definition | Capture |
|---|---|---|
| Unaided completion | Learner finishes the core quest without facilitator help beyond the initial instructions. **In-app hints are allowed**; a verbal or physical hint from the facilitator means “not unaided”. | `firstQuestUnaided` |
| Core duration | From tapping the start of the core quest to opening the chest. Exclude consent, setup, and the fairness conversation. | `coreDurationSeconds` |
| Fairness | “How fair did the app’s feedback feel?” 1 = very unfair, 5 = very fair. Ask once, immediately after the first quest, without leading. | `fairness1to5` |
| Next-day recall | Learner writes 人/大/天 **from memory on paper**, without the app, within 60 seconds. Count a character if it is recognizable and the stroke order is broadly correct; do not correct during the recall. | `nextDayRecallCount` |
| Third session | Any later session with at least one graded write. Record the date. | `thirdSessionDate` |
| Incident | Crash, lost progress, duplicate reward, or a core journey that cannot continue. Record what happened and the steps. | `incidents` |

Do not derive “unaided” from the verdict stream. A learner can use an in-app hint and
still be unaided by the facilitator; a facilitator can quietly point at the screen and
leave no trace in the app. The observer’s judgement is the measurement.

## 8. Data capture and retention

- Fill one worksheet row per participant in `data/pilot/pilot-results.csv` (copy the
  template; the real file is git-ignored).
- Keep signed consent/assent forms **separate** from the worksheet, in the hosting
  organization’s locked storage. Do not photograph them into the repo.
- The worksheet contains no names, no audio, no video, and no free-form identifying
  details. Use the `notes` column for context that matters to the analysis, not for
  identifying information.
- Raw notes are destroyed after the report is reviewed. Proposed retention: **90 days
  after the gate decision**, or sooner on request. The hosting organization’s policy wins
  if it is stricter.
- On withdrawal: stop, remove the participant’s row, and record only that a withdrawal
  occurred (no ID) in the facilitator notes. A withdrawal is not a failure.

## 9. Analysis and gate decision

```bash
./gradlew :data-ingest:analyzePilotResults   # writes data/pilot/pilot-report.md
./gradlew :data-ingest:checkPilotResults     # exits non-zero unless every gate passes
```

The gate requires:

- **5–8 complete participants** (guardian consent, teen assent, all three sessions);
- **≥80% finish unaided**;
- **median core duration ≤10 minutes**;
- **≥80% rate fairness ≥4/5**;
- **≥80% write two of 人/大/天 next day**;
- **every complete participant’s third session is 1–4 days after the first quest**;
- **zero incidents** (crash, lost progress, duplicate reward, blocked core journey).

If a threshold fails, do not move the threshold. Record the failure, decide whether to
iterate on the product, and run a new pilot with a fresh protocol version. The report is
evidence for the release gate; it is not a substitute for the teacher-content, corpus,
mockup, or license gates.

## 10. Stop and withdrawal rules

Stop the session immediately if:

- the learner is distressed, frustrated, or asks to stop;
- the guardian asks to stop;
- a crash or data loss blocks the core journey;
- a privacy or safeguarding concern arises.

The app has no attempt limit and no failure state; stopping is always allowed. Do not
offer rewards or pressure to continue.

## 11. Incident response

1. Stop the core journey; do not make the learner repeat a broken step more than once.
2. Record the time, screen, and steps in `incidents`. A screenshot is useful; do not
   include the learner’s face or name.
3. If the app crashed, capture `adb logcat` on a facilitator-owned debug device if
   available. Do not install a debug build on the participant’s phone just to collect a
   log.
4. Preserve the phone’s app data until the maintainer says otherwise; do not reset
   progress.
5. Report safeguarding concerns through the hosting organization’s process, not the app.

## 12. Open decisions (resolve before enrollment)

- Which organization hosts the pilot, and whose consent/assent template and safeguarding
  policy apply?
- Which jurisdiction’s consent requirements apply, and who reviews the forms?
- Distribution channel and installer (GitHub Release asset, direct install, MDM)?
- Reimbursement or compensation, if any?
- Data custodian and the final retention period (proposed: 90 days).
- Who runs the corpus phase, on which debug devices, and when?
- Who signs the final gate report (facilitator + maintainer), and where is it stored?

## Appendix A — Guardian consent checklist (template, not legal advice)

Have the hosting organization review this for its jurisdiction. At minimum, the consent
should cover:

- the purpose (a first-ten-minutes usability pilot, not a course or an assessment);
- what the teen will do (one quest, a next-day recall on paper, a third session);
- what is recorded (pseudonymous worksheet: dates, duration, fairness rating, recall
  count, incidents) and what is **not** recorded (name, audio, video, account, network
  data);
- that the app is offline and creates no account;
- that participation is voluntary and can be stopped at any time, with no penalty;
- the foreseeable risks (frustration, time, screen use) and the benefits (none promised);
- who can access the worksheet, where consent forms are stored, and the retention period;
- a contact for questions, withdrawal, or a concern;
- guardian signature/date and a separate teen assent signature/date.

## Appendix B — Teen assent script

> We’re testing a new app that teaches three Chinese characters. We want to know whether
> the first ten minutes make sense on your own. There are no grades and no wrong answers
> about you — we’re testing the app, not you. You can stop at any time, and nobody will be
> annoyed. We’ll write down only a made-up ID, the date, how long it took, how fair it
> felt, and how many characters you remembered the next day. We won’t record your name,
> your voice, or your face, and the app won’t send anything over the internet. Do you have
> questions? Do you want to take part?

Record assent separately; do not treat silence as assent.

## Appendix C — Facilitator script

**Setup:** confirm consent/assent, airplane mode, clean progress, audio, one phone per
participant.

**Intro:** “Today you’ll try the first quest. I’m going to watch and take notes, but I
won’t help unless something is broken. The app gives hints if you get stuck — using them
is fine. You can stop whenever you want.”

**First quest:** start the timer at the start tap. Do not point, mouth, or correct. Note
any facilitator help immediately; it changes `firstQuestUnaided` to `no`.

**After the chest:** stop the timer. Ask exactly: “How fair did the app’s feedback feel,
from 1 = very unfair to 5 = very fair?” Record the answer. Do not explain the scale
beyond that.

**Next day:** give paper and a pen. “Please write 人, 大, and 天 from memory. You have 60
seconds. Don’t worry about being perfect; just do your best.” Do not show the app. Count
recognizable characters with broadly correct stroke order.

**Third session:** let the learner use the app normally. Record the date. Do not add a
session just to reach the gate.

**Close:** thank the learner, confirm they can withdraw, and remind the guardian how to
contact you.
